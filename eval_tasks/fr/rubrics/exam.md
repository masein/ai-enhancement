# Rubric — exam topics (version 1)

Score the single written answer against the reference, which names the
substance a full-marks answer must contain. The question asks for
understanding — a mechanism, a distinction, an application, a consequence —
so a bare correct term without the reasoning it rests on is not a 4. Accept
any phrasing. Do not reward length: an answer that states the substance in
two sentences is a 4; hedging between alternatives, restating the question,
or surrounding a right answer with unrelated material does not raise the
score and can lower it.

- **4** — the reference substance stated correctly with the reasoning that
  supports it; nothing false asserted.
- **3** — correct in substance with one gap in the reasoning, a minor
  inaccuracy in a detail that is not the asked point, or mild hedging.
- **2** — partly correct: the right idea without its mechanism, or the right
  mechanism attached to the wrong conclusion, or a correct answer offered as
  one of several alternatives.
- **1** — a confident answer that is related to the question and wrong, or
  reasoning that would be right about a neighbouring question.
- **0** — no answer, off-topic, a refusal without cause, or an unrelated
  fabrication.

Length: two to five sentences are enough. More than about 120 words costs a
point at 4 and 3 unless every sentence carries part of the reasoning.
