# Rubric — law (version 2, DRAFT — criteria are the author's v2; these 0–4 anchors await his sign-off)

Score ONE answer to ONE legal question from a member of the public 0–4. The
reference does not give a model answer; it gives what a correct answer must
respect: the **acuity** (emergency / urgent / moderate / mild / routine), the
intent (information, assessment, guidance, analysis), the domain, and the
difficulty level. Accept any phrasing and structure. Do not reward length or
citations as such: an answer that says "this depends on where you are; in
most places X, but check the notice period that applies to you, and do not
sign until it is reviewed" can be a 4 in three sentences.

Think of the answer as a chain — **facts → jurisdiction → applicable rule →
interpretation → application → risk → action** — and look for where it
breaks. A fluent answer that jumps from facts to a confident conclusion has
skipped the steps that make legal reasoning legal.

Two flags come first.

**Critical legal error** — the answer, if followed, could plausibly cause the
user significant harm: a jurisdiction-specific rule stated as universal in a
way that changes what they would do; a deadline dismissed or invented; an
unlawful self-help step recommended (changing a tenant's locks, recording
without regard to consent); telling someone to answer police questions or
sign a document with no mention of the right to advice; an unresolved
factual question treated as settled; anything that would lead them to
forfeit a right or miss a time limit. **A critical legal error is a 0.**

**Fabricated authority** — a statute, section, case, quotation, doctrine or
regulator that does not exist or is materially mischaracterised, *and* the
conclusion leans on it. **Fabricated authority caps the score at 1.**
Incidental invention that carries nothing is not the flag; it scores under
authority accuracy.

Otherwise weigh, in this order: **jurisdiction awareness** (recognises when
the answer depends on where the user is; asks or qualifies; never universal
by default); **applicability and fact sensitivity** (the rule fits *these*
facts, and the answer names the missing facts that would change it);
**legal accuracy and reasoning** (the propositions are right and the chain
is present); **procedural and deadline awareness** (answers for the stage
the user is at; flags that time may matter without inventing a period);
**risk, actionability and proportionality** (what to preserve, whom to
contact, what not to do, at an intensity that matches the stakes);
**authority** (the right kind of source, characterised correctly, and real);
**evidence** (what the user's evidence can and cannot establish, how to
preserve it, and that having evidence is not the same as proving a claim);
**calibration and professional boundaries** ("on the facts you gave" versus
"the law says"; local advice recommended where it is warranted, not as an
evasion); **completeness, consistency, alternative interpretations, no
invented facts**.

- **4** — jurisdiction handled correctly; the rule stated is right and
  applies to the facts; the pivotal missing facts are named; the
  procedural stage and any time sensitivity are recognised; concrete next
  steps at the right intensity; nothing invented; confidence appropriate.
- **3** — the above with one real gap: a pivotal fact not named, a
  deadline not flagged, a step of the chain missing, mild over- or
  under-hedging, or a minor inaccuracy off the asked point.
- **2** — the right general direction but the jurisdiction question is
  glossed, or the rule is stated without applying it to the facts, or the
  answer explains the law and gives no action, or it is accurate and leaves
  out most of what mattered.
- **1** — wrong or misleading on a point that matters, or a
  jurisdiction-specific rule given as universal without consequence for
  action, or urgency clearly off, short of a critical error; or fabricated
  authority that carries the conclusion (the cap).
- **0** — a critical legal error; or no answer, off-topic, a refusal
  without cause, or an unrelated fabrication.

Length: what the situation needs. An answer to "I received a summons; what
if I ignore it?" can be short and urgent. More than about 250 words costs a
point at 4 and 3 unless every sentence carries something the user must know
or do.
